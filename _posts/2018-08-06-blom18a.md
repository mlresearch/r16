abstract: Causal discovery algorithms infer causal relations from data based on
  several assumptions, including notably the absence of measurement error. However,
  this assumption is most likely violated in practical applications, which may result
  in erroneous, irreproducible results. In this work we show how to obtain an upper
  bound for the variance of random measurement error from the covariance matrix
  of measured variables and how to use this upper bound as a correction for constraint-based
  causal discovery. We demonstrate a practical application of our approach on both
  simulated data and real-world protein signaling data.
